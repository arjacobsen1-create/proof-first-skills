# Email Absence Claims

## The rule

"No reply" is a finding, and findings need coverage. A message your
search did not return is not the same as a message that does not
exist.

## Earn the absence before you report it

- Search every mailbox the message could plausibly sit in before
  reporting an absence, including archived and non-inbox mail.
- Vary the search: by sender, by subject words, by date range. One
  keyword pass is not a sweep.
- Name any mailbox you could not check. If a check was bounded,
  failed or skipped, the result is partial and must be presented
  as partial.

## Account for index lag

- Recent mail is the highest risk for a false absence: a message
  the person can already see on their phone may not appear in a
  mailbox search for tens of minutes after it arrives.
- When you report a recent message as not found, say plainly that
  the search index may lag behind their own inbox view, so they
  can weigh the result.
- Hold any record change that depends on the absence, such as
  closing an item as unanswered, until the message is found or the
  lag window and coverage make the absence credible.

## Phrase it as coverage, not as fact

- Report "not found in the accounts and searches covered," and name
  that coverage in the same reply. Never report "there is no
  email" or "no reply has arrived."

## Treat the person's own view as the live source

- When a screenshot, forwarded copy or on-screen detail the person
  supplies contradicts your search, work from their evidence:
  extract the sender, subject, date and reference first.
- Accept the correction without defending the earlier absence
  claim, then rerun the search with the exact values from their
  evidence to find the message and any siblings from the same
  sender.
- If the search still cannot surface it, restate the corrected
  finding from their evidence and say the search has not caught
  up, rather than disputing what they can see.
