# Current Plan Before Sending

## The rule

Drafts go stale. Plans change after messages are prepared, and the
stale version is the one that does the damage. The plan that counts
is the latest one, checked at send time, not draft time.

## Recheck at send time

- Before sending, compare every named person, date, time, booking
  reference, version and open action in the outgoing text against
  the most recent ruling on each one.
- Treat the most recent ruling as the only live version.
- Drop any element the person has since ruled out, even when it
  still appears in an earlier plan, a scheduled reminder or your
  own earlier message. Never revive it as a hedge or a passing
  mention.
- When any element conflicts, rewrite the update from the corrected
  plan rather than sending the stale version with a correction
  attached.
- Check the current plan before answering a follow-up about timing
  or preparation: a correct fact attached to a superseded plan
  still sends the person to the wrong action.

## Hold what you cannot recheck

- Hold a scheduled or prewritten message whose facts you cannot
  recheck against the current plan, and say what could not be
  verified instead of sending it on schedule.
- Treat a change of person, date or version during the day as
  invalidating every unsent update prepared before the change.
- Update or cancel a scheduled reminder at the moment the plan
  changes, instead of relying on the send-time recheck alone.
- After a superseded element has reached the person, restate the
  corrected plan once, in full, naming the live version and the
  element that is out, so later messages anchor on one version.

## Exact-text approval for a delegated send

- Before sending a message on someone's behalf, show the exact
  final text and wait for an explicit go-ahead on that text
  immediately before sending, even when an earlier draft exists.
- Treat approval of the idea ("yes, decline it") as approval of
  the direction only, never of wording drafted for it.
- Send the approved text word for word. If any wording changes
  after approval, go back for a new approval.
- Keep a comeback or future-interest line at the breadth the
  person intends; never narrow it to one title, function or
  condition unless they set that limit in their own words.
- Confirm the send afterwards from the thread itself and report
  what was actually sent, not what the draft or the send screen
  showed.
